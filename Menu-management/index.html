02Objectives

Less time for staff. No more phones per brand.

For outlet staff

For the business

  • Replace the third party POS with an in house solution to cut ongoing platform fees
03The process

I went to the outlet to watch it happen.

Understanding the current flow

How do staff usually manage the menu?

  • Per menu, per reason?
  • Multiple menus in one brand, per reason?
  • Multiple menus in multiple brands, per reason?
  • Multiple menus in multiple brands, with multiple reasons?

The requirements from the PM weren't detailed enough, so I gathered them myself from the Ops team and asked for the documents they use to manage menus.

Findings

Staff turn off multiple menus across multiple brands for one reason. They need to be able to:

  1. Turn on or off one menu in one brand on one platform
  2. Turn on or off multiple menus in one brand on one platform
  3. Turn on or off multiple menus in multiple brands on one platform
  4. Turn on or off multiple menus in multiple brands on multiple platforms
04Context

Three words staff already used.

Materials

Physical items or ingredients needed to prepare or serve menu items. Their availability decides whether related menus can be offered.

Parent menu

The main dish on the menu. It can have variants or sizes, the child menus, attached to it.

Child menu

A variant of a parent menu, like a size, flavour or customization. It depends on the parent and on materials.

05Design

Designed for a 1024 × 768 POS screen.

Small screen

1024 × 768 meant a compact, scannable layout.

Poor display

High contrast colours that stay clear on weak screens.

Familiar words

Parent menu became Menu, child menu became Kompleks menu, the terms staff knew.

Final UI

The child menu module, after internal testing and an outlet trial. It shipped to all Hangry outlets only after those rounds of feedback.
